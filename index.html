New Business Line Development

From Waste Dump to Sellable Nickel

Building an internal mining contractor business line to unlock low-grade nickel value at lower operating cost.

Value Creation
Operational Output 3.6 million tons Low-grade nickel material moved and sold.
Cost Control US$2 per ton Actual rehandling cost monitored below plan and benchmark.
Business Impact Lower cost Reduced dependency on higher-cost external contractors.
Business Situation

The company relied on external contractors for mining operations. A large volume of low-grade nickel had been treated as waste, but HPAL market growth created a new opportunity. The material could only be profitable if rehandling cost stayed low.

What I Worked On

Worked on business plan, feasibility logic, CAPEX/OPEX planning, owning and operating cost, cost-per-ton structure, payback view, execution scheduling, SOP, field monitoring, and stakeholder coordination.

What Changed

The company was able to move and sell around 3.6 million tons of material, reduce dependency on higher-cost contractors, and monitor actual rehandling cost at around US$2 per ton.

Reporting System Improvement

From Manual Reports to Clear Business Visibility

Making reports faster, clearer, and easier for management to use.

Reporting
Process Efficiency 4 hours to 5 minutes Reporting process improved into faster business visibility.
Data Visibility Realtime input Data linked from operational input into dashboard and report.
Decision Support Faster signals Management could identify abnormalities faster.
Business Situation

Reports existed, but the process was slow and the output was not always easy for management to read. Abnormalities in field performance were not clearly visible, and it was difficult to quickly understand whether performance needed action.

What I Worked On

Improved data flow by cutting repeated admin input, moving operational input closer to real time, linking Excel/Google Sheets, and building dashboard connections using Looker Studio, AppSheet, Power BI, Tableau, and query-based reporting.

What Changed

Reporting improved from 4 hours to 5 minutes, while management could see key indicators, identify abnormalities faster, and use reports as clearer decision-support tools.

Company Operating System Improvement

Building the Company Operating System

Turning unclear processes, scattered documents, and slow approvals into clearer governance and execution flow.

Governance
Decision Cycle 5 days to 1 day Decision cycle improved through clearer governance.
Operating System System built SOP, KPI, document control, quality manual, and approval flow.
Accountability Clearer ownership Teams gained clearer roles, documents, and follow-up rhythm.
Business Situation

After almost five years of operation, the company still had unclear business processes, inconsistent SOP, scattered internal documents, and slow approval flow. Projects often moved without clear ownership.

What I Worked On

Worked on a clearer company operating system by combining Balanced Scorecard, OKR, and ISO 9001:2015-based management system implementation into governance, SOP, KPI alignment, document control, and quality manual structure.

Teams gained clearer direction, better document control, stronger process ownership, and more measurable activities. Decision cycle improved from 5 days to 1 day.

New Business Execution Setup

Building a Trading Arm to Capture More Margin

Preparing the resources, structure, reporting, and coordination needed to execute a new business opportunity more sustainably.

Commercial Execution
Revenue Impact US$2.8 million Monthly revenue reached within 3 months.
Margin Impact US$200 thousand Approximate monthly margin generated.

04 · From Business Direction to Execution Control

How the work usually moves.

Across projects and daily business execution, my work usually follows one flow: understand the direction, define the scope and numbers, build the execution plan, align the teams, monitor progress with clear signals, and improve the system based on what happens in the field.

How the work usually moves

From business direction into scope, schedule, execution roadmap, team alignment, monitoring signals, and continuous improvement.

Understand Scope Plan Align Monitor Improve
01

Understand the Business Direction

I clarify what the company wants to achieve, what problem needs to be solved, what milestone needs to move, and what decision needs to be supported.

02

Define the Scope, Schedule, and Business Numbers

I turn the direction into something more practical: project scope, assumptions, timeline, resource needs, cost structure, CAPEX/OPEX, cash flow, budget vs actual logic, payback view, risks, and escalation points.

03

Build the Execution Roadmap

I break the plan into priorities, milestones, SOP, workflow, ownership, KPI, and follow-up rhythm — so teams know what needs to happen first, who owns it, and how progress should be tracked.

04

Align the Teams Who Need to Execute

I connect leadership, finance, operations, commercial, procurement, and field teams so everyone understands the same goal, scope, numbers, priorities, and responsibilities.

05

Monitor Execution with Clear Signals

I build or improve reporting, dashboards, KPI tracking, and actual-vs-plan monitoring as part of the execution control system — so leaders can act faster when progress slows down, cost moves off-track, or performance becomes abnormal.

06

Improve the System Based on Execution Feedback

I use findings from reports, dashboards, team feedback, and field conditions to improve the way the work runs — through SOP refinement, process improvement, clearer ownership, better controls, or continuous improvement initiatives.
